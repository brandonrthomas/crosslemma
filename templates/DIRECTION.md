# Direction brief — @SLUG@: ⟨the direction in one line⟩

**Header notes** (newest first; each dated, its text approved by the user; the body below them is never
edited). ⟨A header note corrects the body by pointing at the record — a register entry, a claim id, an audit —
and says what it changes and what it leaves standing.⟩

1. ⟨date, `session`: what was learned that the body did not know; read, not certified.⟩

---

## 1. Why the direction is changing

⟨What the record shows about the previous direction, by claim id and attack status. What it does not show.⟩

## 2. The problem, stated with no shapes in it

⟨The question in the form the direction attacks, stripped of the particular cases the earlier work chose.⟩

## 3. Filters every proposal must pass

⟨The tests of `PROBLEM.md` §5, and any the direction adds. A candidate that fails one is out before a run.⟩

## 4. Candidate mechanisms

### M1. ⟨name⟩
⟨What it is; why it could cover the whole question at once; what it needs; what would kill it.⟩

⟨…⟩

## 5. The author's ranking, to be attacked

⟨Which first and why. This ranking is the author's opinion and is the first thing an outside audit should try
to overturn.⟩

## 6. Proposed way to begin

⟨A scout, not a solver, is the first run. What it receives, what it must report, its kill criteria.⟩

## 7. What no session in this workspace may do

⟨Standing prohibitions specific to this direction: texts no worker may see, results not to be cited as
certified, the neighbour that is read-only.⟩

## 8. Recollections to confirm before anyone leans on them

⟨Every fact the author states from memory, listed so it can be sourced or struck.⟩

## 9. Opening prompt

⟨The prompt the user pastes to start the first session on this direction.⟩

## 10. Disclosure

⟨What the author read to write this, and what it did not.⟩
